What to have ready and what to avoid

You can prepare for the conversation without trying to inspect or diagnose the roof yourself. Gather any information you already have about the concern, and write down questions so you don’t have to remember them all on the spot. If there are details you’re uncertain about, mark them as uncertain rather than presenting a guess as fact.

Use this preparation list:
- Keep the property address handy and identify the part of the home involved, if you know it.
- Note when you first noticed the concern and any changes since then.
- List questions about the work, its scope, and how decisions will be discussed.
- Ask what information the contractor needs before explaining a proposed approach.
- Review the details and request clarification before you agree to anything.

One mistake to avoid is treating a symptom as proof of a particular roofing problem. Another is accepting a description you don’t understand just to move the conversation along. If you see a new concern or don’t know whether it needs professional attention, call a roofing contractor and describe it. Avoid taking on roof work that you aren’t prepared to do safely.
